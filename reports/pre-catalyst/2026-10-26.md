## 1A. Ratings and realistic predictions (added 2026-10-26, about 15:30 ET)

**How ratings are set:**
- Each name gets 2–4 explicit scenarios over a **6-month horizon (to 2027-04-26)**, each with a probability and a price target.
- **Expected return** is the probability-weighted average of those targets, measured against today's price.
- **Rating bands:**
  - **Strong Buy:** at least +20%, provided no scenario with 15%+ probability loses more than 15%, and confidence is medium-high or better.
  - **Buy:** at least +8%.
  - **Hold:** between −5% and +8%.
  - **Sell:** −5% or worse.
  - **Strong Sell:** −15% or worse.
  - OTC, illiquid and heavy-dilution names can never be rated Strong Buy or Strong Sell.
- **How the probabilities are anchored:**
  - **Published base rates.** Roughly one-third of press-reported takeover rumours become an announced deal within a year (Ahern & Sosyura, *Journal of Finance*, 2015). Announced definitive US deals have historically completed about 90% or more of the time (merger-arbitrage literature, e.g. Mitchell & Pulvino, 2001). Activist 13D targets earn about +7% abnormal returns around filing, concentrated in targets that are later sold (Brav et al., 2008; Greenwood & Schor, 2009).
  - **The market-implied probability.** This is where today's price sits between the "no deal" price and the "deal" price. I only depart from it by more than about 15 percentage points when I have specific evidence.
- **Price ranges** are statistical 80% bands for the next 3 months (to 2027-01-26). They are calculated from each stock's 1-year realized volatility, assuming lognormal returns. They are not used for announced-deal names, whose volatility collapses once the deal is signed.
- **The probabilities are my judgement and are not yet calibrated.** Each prediction is logged in `candidate_log.csv` with a deadline. Later runs will score them (Brier score for the event probabilities; hit rate for the 80% ranges, which should be about 80%).
